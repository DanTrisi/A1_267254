\section{Walking with double support}
\textbf{Weight transfer.} In single support the stance foot carries the whole weight ($\approx mg$, $\pm4\%$ due to the vertical acceleration of the CoM, $f_N = m(g+\ddot c_z)$) and the swing foot 0\,N (\fig{q1ds}, \fig{q1nods}). The reference CoP stays under the stance foot ($y=\pm0.096$\,m) and, \colorbox{yellow}{with two contacts, is the average of the feet positions weighted by the normal forces, $p_{CoP} = (f_Lp_L+f_Rp_R)/(f_L+f_R)$, so $f_L = mg\,(p_{CoP}-p_R)/(p_L-p_R)$: each force is a linear function of the CoP. With double support (DS) the CoP moves smoothly from one foot to the other in 0.2\,s and the weight follows gradually (RF $373\!\to\!35$\,N, LF $29\!\to\!358$\,N);} without DS the CoP jumps by 19.2\,cm in one 2\,ms step and the whole weight ($\approx390$\,N) is transferred in one step (\fig{q1zoom}). With a single contact the CoP must stay inside that sole, so without a phase with two contacts it can only jump. Hence the max normal force rate is 11 times larger without DS (195\,kN/s vs 17.8\,kN/s) and so is the joint torque rate (28.8 vs 6.3\,kNm/s). With DS the residual rate comes from two small jumps left by \code{transition\_time = 0} ($\approx$29\,N at touchdown, $\approx$35\,N at lift-off).

\textbf{Real robot.} Without DS the walk works only because the simulation is ideal: the state is integrated from the QP accelerations (the robot does exactly what TSID computes), contacts are rigid and instantaneous and the touchdown time is exact. On a real robot torques cannot change in 2\,ms (actuator bandwidth), the swing foot would take the whole weight during the impact, an early/late touchdown (model errors, uneven ground) would transfer the weight to a foot not yet on the ground, and noisy, delayed force sensors cannot confirm the contact so quickly.

\textbf{Fall with \code{transition\_time = 0.1} and fix.} \code{removeRigidContact(name, 0.1)} keeps the contact for 0.1\,s while decreasing linearly its maximum normal force $f_{max}$ to 0, then removes it; the ramp leaves $f_{max}=0$ inside the \code{Contact6d}, and \code{addRigidContact} re-adds the same object without restoring it. In \fig{q1fall} the left foot is removed at the start ($f_{max}^{LF}\to0$ at 1.4\,s); at touchdown (2.5\,s) the constraint $0\le f_N\le f_{max}=0$ forbids it to carry any load, so when the right foot is lifted the robot falls (2.81\,s). Fix: \code{contactRF/LF.setMaxNormalForce(conf.fMax)} in \code{add\_contact\_RF/LF} before \code{addRigidContact}; the walk is then completed (\fig{q1fixed}). \textbf{The ramp makes little difference} with this gait: same CoM error (2.9/6.8\,mm RMS/max), force rate $17.8\to15.0$\,kN/s, torque rate $6.3\to4.6$\,kNm/s. The weight is already transferred by the DS through the CoP: when the ramp starts (2.6\,s) the actual force is already below it, so it is active only in the last $\approx$20\,ms, removing the 35\,N jump at lift-off (\fig{q1ramp}); the largest jump left ($\approx$30\,N) is at touchdown, which the ramp does not affect. (Without DS the ramp makes the robot fall even with the fix: the only stance foot would be unloaded.)

\section{Tray along a sinusoid}
Hand task: \code{TaskSE3Equality}, $K_p=10$, $K_d=2\sqrt{K_p}$, mask and frame from the conf, added only if \code{w\_hand>0} or level 0. Reference: $p=c+A\sin\omega t$, $\dot p = A\omega\cos\omega t$, $\ddot p=-A\omega^2\sin\omega t$.

\textbf{Without feedforward} $\dot p^{ref}=\ddot p^{ref}=0$ and only $\ddot p^d = K_p(p^{ref}-p)-K_d\dot p$ is left: the hand moves only because of the position error, so it lags behind and moves less. It is a critically damped low-pass filter $P/P^{ref}=K_p/(s^2+K_ds+K_p)$ with $\omega_n=\sqrt{K_p}\approx0.5$\,Hz: at 0.5\,Hz ($=\omega_n$) theory gives 0.50/89.6$^\circ$, at 2\,Hz ($\approx4\omega_n$, attenuation $\propto K_p/\omega^2$) 0.06/151.8$^\circ$; a frequency sweep matches the theory (\fig{q2freq}, \fig{q2track}). \textbf{With feedforward} ideally $\ddot e+K_d\dot e+K_pe=0$ (perfect tracking): the lag is indeed small ($\le10^\circ$) but the amplitude drops at high frequency. The reason is the CoM: the base is floating, so accelerating the arm and tray pushes the rest of the body (momentum conservation) and moves the CoM, while the CoM task, with the same weight ($w_{com}=w_{hand}=1$), wants to keep it still; the QP compromises. The required acceleration $A\omega^2$ is 16 times larger at 2\,Hz (7.9 vs 0.49\,m/s$^2$): the CoM moves by 5.5\,cm (vs 7\,mm) and drifts, the hand oscillates around a lower height, and the right ankle reaches its torque limit (\fig{q2com}, \fig{q2track}).

\section{Walking with the tray}
Hand reference: initial height and orientation, $x$-$y$ = initial hand position + displacement of the CoM reference, velocity/acceleration = those of the CoM reference (zero along $z$). With \code{w\_hand}=1 instead of 0: max tilt $12.8^\circ\to0.04^\circ$, hand error $66\to9$\,mm, height range $98\to14$\,mm, but CoM error $2.9/6.8\to7.1/14.4$\,mm (RMS/max), mostly along $y$ in single support (\fig{q3hand}, \fig{q3com}). The cause is not the mass of the arm but the kinematic coupling: the hand pose depends on legs, pelvis and torso, the same DOFs used by the CoM task. Walking makes the body sway ($\pm8$\,cm laterally) and, without the hand task, this sway reaches the tray (12.8$^\circ$, 10\,cm); keeping the hand still requires counteracting the upper-body motion, which also moves the CoM. With equal weights the QP sacrifices part of the CoM tracking, and the 6D hand task leaves less redundancy to the CoM task.

Position only: the orientation is free and the tray tilts up to 22$^\circ$. Orientation only: the tray is horizontal but the hand moves by 86\,mm with the body. The waiter needs constant height and no rotation about the horizontal axes, while $x$, $y$ and the yaw are free: mask $[0,0,1,1,1,0]$, a requirement defined in the \emph{world} frame (\fig{q4time}, \fig{q4sum}). In the world-aligned frame the mask selects exactly height and tilt: the requirement is met as with the full mask (0.03$^\circ$, 10.5\,mm) with half the CoM error (3.6 vs 7.1\,mm), since only 3 DOFs are constrained. In the local frame the same mask selects components along the hand axes, which are not aligned with the world (the hand $z$ axis is 53$^\circ$ from the vertical) and rotate with the hand: freeing $r_z$ frees part of the tilt and freeing $x$-$y$ frees part of the vertical motion (8.9$^\circ$, 78\,mm). With the full mask the frame is irrelevant (identical results): the error is the same vector in different axes and, with equal gains, its norm does not change.

\section{Heavy trays}
\textbf{5 kg tray} (\fig{q5pelvis}): the robot shifts its pelvis by $\approx$3.5\,cm to the left and 2.3\,cm backwards for the whole walk (leaning away from the tray, which is in front-right of the pelvis), while the total CoM (robot + tray) is unchanged within a few mm. The payload is in the model, so the CoM task controls the CoM of robot + tray with the same reference; to compensate the 5\,kg at $\approx$0.31\,m to the right, the CoM of the robot alone moves by 2.7\,cm left and 1.8\,cm back, mainly by moving the pelvis. The hand task ($w_{hand}=100$) keeps the tray in place, so the posture task, with the lowest weight, gives way.

\textbf{2 kg tray, nominal limits} (\fig{q5tau}, \fig{q5tray}): with the hand task as a hard constraint the QP becomes infeasible at 2.23\,s, because it conflicts with the \textbf{torque bounds}, in particular of \code{RWristPitch}, the joint carrying the tray (limit $0.6\times1.45=0.87$\,Nm), already at 90\% when standing and at 100\% just before the failure (right knee at 91\%). With $w_{hand}=100$ the walk is completed: the wrist stays at its limit for 1.24\,s and the QP sacrifices hand tracking instead (hand dropping by 13\,mm at each step, error up to 32\,mm vs 0.4\,mm with the empty tray, tilt 0.18$^\circ$) and slightly the CoM (8.5 vs 7.6\,mm RMS). \textbf{Hard constraints} should be used for what is physically mandatory and always satisfiable (dynamics, contacts, torque/joint limits): if two of them become incompatible the QP has no solution and the robot falls. Objectives that may conflict with the physics (hand pose, CoM, posture) should be weighted costs: a large weight gives almost strict priority when feasible but lets the task degrade gracefully when it is not.
